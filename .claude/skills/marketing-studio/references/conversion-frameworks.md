# Conversion Frameworks Reference

Frameworks and techniques for optimizing conversion rates across marketing assets.

---

## Copywriting Formulas

### PAS (Problem - Agitate - Solve)

1. **Problem**: Name the problem the audience faces
2. **Agitate**: Amplify the pain — what happens if they don't solve it?
3. **Solve**: Present your product as the solution

**Example**:
- Problem: "Finding a trusted Vedic astrologer abroad is nearly impossible."
- Agitate: "Generic horoscope apps use Western tropical astrology — missing Nakshatras, Dashas, and everything that makes Jyotish meaningful."
- Solve: "JyotishAI uses Swiss Ephemeris Vedic calculations with AI interpretation — the accuracy of a jyotishi, available instantly."

---

### AIDA (Attention - Interest - Desire - Action)

1. **Attention**: Hook with a bold headline or surprising fact
2. **Interest**: Build engagement with relevant details
3. **Desire**: Create wanting through benefits and social proof
4. **Action**: Drive a specific action with a clear CTA

**Best for**: Landing pages, email copy, sales pages

---

### 4U's (Useful - Urgent - Unique - Ultra-Specific)

Headlines should be at least 3 of these 4:

- **Useful**: Does it promise a benefit?
- **Urgent**: Is there a reason to act now?
- **Unique**: Is it different from everything else?
- **Ultra-Specific**: Does it use concrete details, not vagueness?

**Example**:
- Weak: "Astrology App" (only slightly useful)
- Strong: "Get Your Vedic Birth Chart with AI Interpretation in 30 Seconds — Free" (useful, unique, ultra-specific)

---

### BAB (Before - After - Bridge)

1. **Before**: Describe the world with the problem
2. **After**: Describe the world with the problem solved
3. **Bridge**: Explain how your product gets them from Before to After

**Best for**: Short copy, email subject lines, social media

## Landing Page Optimization

### Above-the-Fold Rule

Visitors decide within 5 seconds whether to stay or leave. The above-the-fold area must contain:

1. **What it is** (headline)
2. **Why it matters** (subheadline / value prop)
3. **What to do** (CTA button)
4. **Why trust it** (social proof snippet)

### CTA Best Practices

| Principle | Good | Bad |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Action-oriented verbs | "Get My Free Chart" | "Submit" |
| Benefit in CTA | "Start Saving Time" | "Sign Up" |
| First-person phrasing | "Get My Report" | "Get Your Report" |
| Reduce friction | "No credit card required" | (no risk reducer) |
| Contrast color | Button stands out from page | Button blends in |
| Size and spacing | Large, padded, whitespace around it | Small, cramped, buried |

### Social Proof Hierarchy

Strongest to weakest:

1. **Specific metrics**: "12,847 charts generated" (most credible)
2. **Named testimonials with photo/title**: "Priya M., Software Engineer, SF"
3. **Star ratings with count**: "4.7/5 from 200+ users"
4. **Logos of known customers/partners**: Brand recognition transfer
5. **Generic testimonials**: "This product is great!" (least credible)
6. **"As seen in" press logos**: Media recognition (if authentic)

## Email Optimization

### Subject Line Frameworks

| Framework | Example | When to Use |
|-----------|---------|------------|
| Question | "What does your Nakshatra reveal?" | Curiosity-driven opens |
| Number | "3 things your birth chart says about your career" | List/educational content |
| Personal | "Quick question, {first_name}" | Re-engagement, conversational |
| Urgency | "Your free chart expires in 48 hours" | Time-limited offers |
| Curiosity gap | "We found something unexpected in beta data..." | Newsletters, announcements |
| Social proof | "200+ users discovered this about their charts" | Building FOMO |

### Email Body Principles

1. **One email, one goal, one CTA** — Don't ask the reader to do 3 things
2. **Scannable structure** — Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences), bold key points, bullet lists
3. **Personalization** — Use the reader's name and relevant data (plan, usage, etc.)
4. **Mobile-first** — 60%+ of emails are opened on mobile. Single column, large CTA buttons
5. **P.S. line** — The most-read line after the subject. Use it for your strongest point

## A/B Testing Framework

### What to Test (by impact)

| Priority | Element | Expected Impact |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Headline / subject line | 20-50% conversion difference |
| 2 | CTA text and placement | 10-30% conversion difference |
| 3 | Social proof type | 10-20% conversion difference |
| 4 | Page layout / email format | 5-15% conversion difference |
| 5 | Images / visual style | 5-10% conversion difference |
| 6 | Color / font changes | 1-5% conversion difference |

### Statistical Significance Rules

- **Minimum sample size**: 100 conversions per variant (not just 100 visitors)
- **Significance threshold**: p < 0.05 (95% confidence)
- **Test duration**: Run for at least 7 days (captures day-of-week variation)
- **One variable at a time**: Change only one element per test
- **Document everything**: Record hypothesis, variants, results, and learnings

### Common A/B Testing Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Calling a winner too early | Random variation looks like a signal | Wait for statistical significance |
| Testing too many things at once | Can't attribute results to any single change | Test one variable per experiment |
| Ignoring segment differences | Overall winner may lose in your best segment | Segment results by audience |
| Never testing | You're leaving conversion on the table | Always have at least one test running |
| Only testing small things | Colors and fonts rarely move the needle | Test big things: headlines, offers, pricing |

## Pricing Page Optimization

### Pricing Psychology

| Technique | How It Works | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Anchoring** | Show the expensive option first so others seem reasonable | Display Enterprise → Pro → Basic (right to left) |
| **Decoy effect** | Add a "bad deal" option to make the target option look better | Add a limited tier that's only $5 less than the full tier |
| **Annual discount** | Show monthly vs annual pricing to encourage commitment | "$29/mo or $19/mo billed annually (save 34%)" |
| **Feature highlighting** | Visually emphasize the recommended plan | "Most Popular" badge, highlighted border, larger card |
| **Round numbers** | Round prices feel less calculated | $29/mo feels friendlier than $28.99/mo for SaaS |

### Pricing Page Best Practices

1. Show 3 tiers (max 4) — too many options cause decision paralysis
2. Recommend one tier explicitly ("Most Popular" or "Best Value")
3. Lead with the feature the user cares about most, not the cheapest feature
4. Show annual pricing as monthly amount ("$19/mo billed annually") not total ("$228/year")
5. Include a free tier or free trial to reduce friction
6. End with a FAQ that addresses pricing objections
