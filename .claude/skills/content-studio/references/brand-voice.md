# MDS Brand Voice Guide

## The Core Voice

**In one sentence:** MDS sounds like a smart friend who happens to be an expert — technically precise, naturally conversational, and never corporate.

**The shorthand test:** Before publishing anything, ask: "Would Shrish actually say this out loud to a colleague at a whiteboard?" If not, rewrite it.

---

## Voice Attributes

### 1. Technically Precise
We know our stuff. We use correct terminology. We don't simplify to the point of inaccuracy. When we say "arc-second precision," we mean it. When we cite a cost figure, it's from real data.

**Do:** "Swiss Ephemeris calculates planetary positions to arc-second precision."
**Don't:** "Our super-accurate algorithm does amazing calculations."

### 2. Naturally Conversational
We talk like humans, not press releases. We use contractions. We start sentences with "And" and "But." We ask rhetorical questions. We use dashes for asides — like this.

**Do:** "Here's the thing — most AI products are too expensive to scale."
**Don't:** "It is important to note that the majority of artificial intelligence products face scalability challenges related to cost structures."

### 3. Confidently Humble
We're confident in what we know and honest about what we don't. We share our results without bragging. We acknowledge limitations without apologizing.

**Do:** "We reduced costs by 50x. That said, this approach doesn't work for pure conversational AI."
**Don't:** "We're REVOLUTIONIZING the AI industry with our GROUNDBREAKING approach!!!"

### 4. Teaching, Not Selling
Every piece of content should teach something useful. If someone reads our post and learns something — even if they never buy anything — we've succeeded. The selling happens because the teaching builds trust.

**Do:** "Here's the framework. Here's how to apply it. Here's what to watch out for."
**Don't:** "Schedule a demo to learn our proprietary methodology."

### 5. Specific Over Vague
Specificity is credibility. "$0.003 per reading" is more believable than "dramatically reduced costs." "50,000 calculations" is more impressive than "complex computation." Numbers, names, and details.

**Do:** "$0.003 per reading instead of $0.15. 50x cost advantage."
**Don't:** "Significantly more cost-effective than traditional approaches."

## Vocabulary Guide

### Words We Use
| Word | When |
|------|------|
| Build | For creating products, systems, architectures |
| Ship | For launching, deploying, publishing |
| Framework | For our methodology and approach |
| Studio | For our specialized teams (Content Studio, Design Studio, etc.) |
| Deterministic | For non-AI computation (math, rules, caching) |
| Tier 1/2/3 | For our cost model |
| Architecture | For system design and structure |

### Words We Never Use
| Banned Word | Why | Use Instead |
|-------------|-----|-------------|
| Leverage | Corporate jargon | "use" or be more specific |
| Synergy | Empty buzzword | "working together" or specific collaboration |
| Paradigm | Nobody talks like this | "approach" or "model" |
| Innovative | Self-applied adjective — let others call you innovative | describe the innovation specifically |
| Disruptive | Overused, meaningless | describe the actual change |
| Revolutionary | Hyperbolic | "significantly different" or just show the results |
| Value proposition | MBA-speak | "what you get" or "why it matters" |
| Thought leader | Self-serving label | demonstrate expertise through content instead |
| Best-in-class | Empty superlative | provide specific evidence of quality |
| Cutting-edge | Cliche | describe the actual technology |
| Scalable solution | Every SaaS says this | explain specific scaling characteristics |
| Empower | Vague and overused | "enable," "help," or be specific about the action |
| Holistic | Meaningless modifier | describe what's actually comprehensive |
| Ecosystem | Unless literally referring to an ecology | "system," "platform," or "toolset" |
| Seamless | Nothing is seamless | "smooth," "integrated," or describe the integration |
| Robust | Filler adjective | describe the actual reliability characteristics |

### Phrases We Avoid
| Phrase | Why | Alternative |
|--------|-----|------------|
| "In today's fast-paced world" | Every article starts this way | Jump directly to the point |
| "I'm excited to share" | Filler, reader doesn't care about your excitement | Share the thing directly |
| "Without further ado" | Acknowledges you've been wasting their time | Just proceed |
| "At the end of the day" | Cliche filler | Remove it entirely |
| "It goes without saying" | Then don't say it | Either say it or don't |
| "To be honest" | Implies you're not always honest | Remove — be honest by default |
| "This is a game-changer" | Let the reader decide that | Describe what changed |
| "I'm passionate about" | Show, don't tell | Demonstrate through expertise |
| "Don't miss out" | Desperation marketing | Make the value clear instead |
| "Act now" | Pressure tactics | State the offer, let them decide |

## Tone Variations by Platform

### LinkedIn
**Dial up:** Professional credibility, teaching mode, thought leadership
**Dial down:** Casual slang, humor that might not land in professional context
**Example tone:** Explaining architecture at a conference after-party — smart but relaxed

### X / Twitter
**Dial up:** Sharpness, opinions, punchy delivery
**Dial down:** Length, qualifications, hedging
**Example tone:** The comment that gets screenshot and shared — memorable in few words

### Blog
**Dial up:** Depth, evidence, thoroughness, structure
**Dial down:** Brevity (this is where you CAN go deep)
**Example tone:** A well-written technical article you'd bookmark and share with your engineering team

### Email
**Dial up:** Directness, personalization, respect for inbox time
**Dial down:** Length, self-promotion, generality
**Example tone:** A note from a colleague who has something genuinely useful for you

### Video / Script
**Dial up:** Conversational rhythm, pacing variation, natural speech patterns
**Dial down:** Written-language constructions, long sentences, passive voice
**Example tone:** Explaining at a whiteboard to a small group who asked a good question

## Brand Values in Content

### 1. Deterministic First
We believe in exhausting every non-AI computation method before reaching for an LLM. This isn't just architecture — it's a content philosophy too. Use templates (Tier 1) before AI generation (Tier 3).

### 2. Show the Work
We share our actual methods, numbers, and reasoning. Not just the results, but how we got there. Transparency builds trust faster than polished marketing.

### 3. Respect the Reader
Every piece of content respects the reader's time and intelligence. No fluff, no filler, no manipulation. Earn their attention with value, not tricks.

### 4. Build in Public
We share our process, including the mistakes and iterations. Authenticity comes from showing the real journey, not just the highlight reel.

## Quick Voice Check

Before publishing, run through these five questions:

1. **Would Shrish say this out loud?** If it sounds weird spoken, rewrite it.
2. **Is there a banned word hiding?** Ctrl+F for the banned list.
3. **Can a stranger understand this?** If it requires insider knowledge, add context.
4. **Is it specific?** Replace vague claims with numbers, names, and details.
5. **Does it teach something?** If the reader learns nothing, add value or don't publish.
