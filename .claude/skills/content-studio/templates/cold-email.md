# Cold Email Template

## Description
Fill-in-the-blank templates for cold outreach emails. Designed to be short (under 150 words), personalized, and focused on earning a reply — not making a sale. Each structure serves a different relationship-building approach.

## When to Use
- Reaching out to prospects who don't know you yet
- Requesting partnerships, collaborations, or introductions
- Pitching consulting, services, or products to cold leads
- When every word counts because the recipient owes you nothing

## Template Structures

### Structure A: Observation > Value > Ask

```
Subject: {SUBJECT_LINE — 3-7 words, specific, not salesy}

Hey {FIRST_NAME},

{PERSONALIZED_OBSERVATION — 1 sentence proving you researched them, referencing something specific they did, said, or built}

{RELEVANCE_BRIDGE — 1 sentence connecting their situation to your expertise}

{VALUE_OFFER — 1-2 sentences describing what you can offer them, framed as benefit to THEM not you}

{LOW_FRICTION_ASK — 1 sentence with a simple, easy-to-answer request}

{SIGN_OFF}

P.S. {PS_LINE — optional social proof or additional hook}
```

### Structure B: Problem > Credibility > Solution

```
Subject: {SUBJECT_LINE}

{FIRST_NAME},

{PROBLEM_STATEMENT — 1 sentence naming a problem they likely have, based on research}

{CREDIBILITY — 1-2 sentences showing you've solved this before, with specific result}

{SOLUTION_TEASE — 1 sentence hinting at how you'd approach their situation}

{ASK — direct, low-friction question}

{SIGN_OFF}
```

### Structure C: Mutual Connection > Context > Ask

```
Subject: {SUBJECT_LINE}

Hi {FIRST_NAME},

{MUTUAL_REFERENCE — how you found them or who connects you}

{CONTEXT — 1-2 sentences on why you're reaching out}

{VALUE — what's in it for them}

{ASK}

{SIGN_OFF}
```

### Structure D: Follow-Up (3-5 days after initial email)

```
Subject: Re: {ORIGINAL_SUBJECT}

{FIRST_NAME},

{BRIEF_REFERENCE — 1 sentence referencing the original email}

{NEW_VALUE — add something new: a relevant article, insight, or offer}

{RESTATE_ASK — slightly different framing of the original ask}

{SIGN_OFF}
```

## Variable Definitions

| Variable | Description | Constraints |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| `{SUBJECT_LINE}` | Email subject | 3-7 words, no clickbait, no ALL CAPS |
| `{FIRST_NAME}` | Recipient's first name | Verify correct spelling |
| `{PERSONALIZED_OBSERVATION}` | Proof of research | 1 sentence, specific to THIS person |
| `{RELEVANCE_BRIDGE}` | Connection to your offer | 1 sentence |
| `{VALUE_OFFER}` | What you're offering | 1-2 sentences, framed as their benefit |
| `{LOW_FRICTION_ASK}` | The request | Reply-based preferred ("interested?" is easier than "book a call") |
| `{SIGN_OFF}` | Name and brief identifier | First name + title or company, 1 line |
| `{PS_LINE}` | Post-script hook | Social proof, stat, or secondary value prop |
| `{PROBLEM_STATEMENT}` | Their likely pain point | 1 sentence, research-informed |
| `{CREDIBILITY}` | Your relevant proof | 1-2 sentences with specific numbers |

## Rules
- Total body under 150 words. Under 125 is even better.
- ONE ask per email. Not "reply or book a call or check our site."
- Personalization must be genuine — not "I love your company" but specific reference to something they did.
- Subject line: no "Quick question," "Following up," or "Touching base." Be specific.
- No attachments in cold emails (triggers spam filters).
- No HTML formatting (plain text performs better for cold outreach).
- Send Tuesday-Thursday, 8:00-9:30 AM recipient's timezone.

## Completed Example (Structure A)

```
Subject: Your AI cost problem has a math solution

Hey Sarah,

Saw your tweet about GPT-4 costs eating into Acme's margins as you scale past 10K users — that's a problem we solved last quarter.

We built an architecture that handles 95% of computation without any AI API calls. Same output quality, 50x cheaper.

I sketched out how this might apply to Acme's recommendation engine. Want me to send over the 1-page breakdown?

— Shrish, MDS

P.S. Our last client with a similar stack went from $0.12 to $0.002 per request. Happy to share the case study too.
```

Word count: 89
Subject line: 8 words, specific, mentions their pain
Personalization: References a specific tweet about a specific problem
Ask: Low friction — "Want me to send the breakdown?" (yes/no answer)
P.S.: Social proof with specific numbers

## Completed Example (Structure D — Follow-Up)

```
Subject: Re: Your AI cost problem has a math solution

Sarah,

Circling back on this — totally understand if the timing isn't right.

One thing I forgot to mention: we just published the full architecture breakdown as a blog post. No pitch, just the technical framework. Might be useful for your engineering team regardless.

Here's the link if helpful: [blog link]

Either way, rooting for Acme's scaling journey.

— Shrish
```

Word count: 64
New value: Free resource that helps them even without engaging
Reframed ask: Implicit — reading the blog post is the new lower-friction ask
Tone: Respectful, not desperate, leaves the door open
