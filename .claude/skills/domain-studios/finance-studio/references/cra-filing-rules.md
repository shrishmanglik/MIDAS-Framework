# CRA Filing Rules Reference -- Canadian Tax for Newcomers

## Residency Determination

### Factual Resident vs. Deemed Resident

Canada taxes based on residency, not citizenship. Residency is a factual determination based on significant residential ties.

**Significant Residential Ties** (any ONE of these establishes residency):
- A home (dwelling place) in Canada available for the taxpayer's use
- A spouse or common-law partner residing in Canada
- Dependants residing in Canada

**Secondary Residential Ties** (considered in aggregate):
- Personal property in Canada (furniture, clothing, car)
- Social ties (memberships in clubs, organizations, religious groups)
- Economic ties (Canadian bank accounts, credit cards, brokerage accounts)
- Provincial health insurance coverage
- Canadian driver's license
- Canadian passport
- Canadian mailing address

**Deemed Resident**: Sojourned in Canada for 183 days or more in a calendar year (without establishing factual residency).

### Newcomer (New Resident) Rules

A newcomer is a person who establishes residential ties in Canada and becomes a resident during the tax year.

**Key dates**:
- **Date of entry**: The date residential ties are established (not necessarily the date of physical arrival)
- **Part-year filing**: File a return for the part of the year you were a Canadian resident

## Part-Year Resident Tax Filing

### Income Reporting (Section 114, Income Tax Act)

| Income Type | Report? | Notes |
|------------|---------|-------|
| Canadian employment income (post-arrival) | Yes | Full amount earned after becoming resident |
| Foreign employment income (pre-arrival) | No | Not taxable in Canada if earned while non-resident |
| Foreign employment income (post-arrival) | Yes | Worldwide income taxable after becoming resident |
| Canadian-source income (pre-arrival) | Depends | Rental income, business income from Canada may be taxable via Section 212 withholding or Section 115 |
| Investment income (post-arrival) | Yes | All sources, worldwide |
| Capital gains (post-arrival) | Yes | On dispositions after becoming resident |

### Credit Proration

Non-refundable tax credits for part-year residents are generally prorated based on the number of days resident in Canada:

```
prorated_credit = full_year_credit * (days_resident / days_in_year)
```

For 2024 (leap year): days_in_year = 366

**Credits that are prorated**:
- Basic personal amount ($15,705 for 2024)
- Spouse/common-law amount
- Age amount
- Canada caregiver credit

**Credits NOT prorated** (based on actual amounts):
- CPP/EI contributions (based on actual contributions)
- Tuition tax credit (based on actual fees paid while resident)
- Medical expense credit (based on actual expenses while resident)
- Charitable donations credit (based on actual donations while resident)

## Tax Treaties -- Key Provisions for Newcomers

### Canada-India DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement)
- **Employment income**: Taxable in the country where services are performed. Indian employment income earned before arrival is taxable in India, not Canada.
- **Investment income**: Dividends withholding limited to 15% (25% for portfolio dividends). Interest withholding limited to 15%.
- **Pensions**: Taxable only in the country of residence (Canada after arrival).
- **Capital gains**: Generally taxable in country of residence. Immovable property gains taxed where property is located.

### Canada-US Tax Convention
- **Employment income**: Taxable where performed. De minimis exemption if present less than 183 days and paid by non-resident employer.
- **Pensions**: May be taxed in both countries. US may withhold 15%; Canada provides foreign tax credit.
- **Social Security**: US Social Security received by Canadian resident: 85% taxable in Canada (since 1996 protocol).
- **Capital gains**: Real property taxed where located. Other gains generally taxed only in country of residence.

### Canada-UK Tax Convention
- **Employment income**: Similar to Canada-India; taxed where performed.
- **Pensions**: May be taxed in both countries. UK state pension taxable only in UK (government service pension).
- **Investment income**: Dividends 15%, interest exempt from withholding.

## Newcomer-Specific Obligations and Opportunities

### RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan)
- Contribution room starts accumulating based on Canadian earned income
- First-year newcomers: no RRSP room until the following year (based on prior year's earned income)
- Formula: RRSP room = 18% of prior year's earned income, up to the annual limit ($31,560 for 2024)
- Unused room carries forward indefinitely

### TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account)
- Contribution room accumulates from January 1 of the year the individual becomes a Canadian resident (or turns 18, whichever is later)
- 2024 annual limit: $7,000
- For a mid-year arrival in 2024: full $7,000 room for that year (not prorated)
- Room from prior years does NOT accumulate for time spent as a non-resident

### FHSA (First Home Savings Account)
- Available to Canadian residents 18+ who have not owned a home in Canada
- 2024 annual limit: $8,000 (lifetime limit $40,000)
- Newcomers may be eligible if they meet the criteria

### T1135 -- Foreign Income Verification Statement
- **Threshold**: Total cost of specified foreign property exceeds CAD $100,000 at any time during the year
- **Specified foreign property includes**: Foreign bank accounts, shares of non-resident corporations, real property outside Canada, foreign bonds/debentures, interests in non-resident trusts
- **Excludes**: Personal-use property, property used in active business, interests in registered plans (RRSP, TFSA held in Canadian institutions)
- **Filing deadline**: Same as the income tax return (April 30 or June 15)
- **Penalties**: $25/day late (min $100, max $2,500 per year). Gross negligence penalty up to $12,000. Extends reassessment period.

### Deemed Acquisition (Section 128.1)
- On becoming a Canadian resident, the taxpayer is deemed to have acquired all property at fair market value
- This establishes the cost base for future Canadian capital gains calculations
- Exception: Canadian real property and other "taxable Canadian property" retain their original cost base
- The taxpayer should document FMV of all foreign assets as of the date of arrival

## Filing Deadlines

| Scenario | Deadline | Extension Conditions |
|---------|----------|---------------------|
| Individual T1 return | April 30 of following year | Automatic extension to June 15 if self-employed (but interest runs from April 30) |
| T1135 Foreign Assets | Same as T1 return | No separate extension |
| T1134 Foreign Affiliates | 15 months after fiscal year end | For those with interests in foreign affiliates |
| Payment of balance owing | April 30 | Interest and penalties from May 1 |
| Instalments (if required) | March 15, June 15, Sep 15, Dec 15 | Required if net tax owing exceeds $3,000 in current and either of two prior years |

## Common Audit Triggers for Newcomers

| Trigger | Why CRA Flags It | How to Mitigate |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Large deposits in Canadian bank accounts early in residency | Potential unreported income | Document source (savings transfers, asset sales from home country) |
| Real property purchases disproportionate to reported income | Raises income source questions | Be prepared to demonstrate source of funds |
| Missing T1135 when high foreign income reported | Inconsistency between income and asset reporting | Always file T1135 if foreign assets exceed $100,000 |
| Large moving expense claims | CRA routinely reviews these | Keep detailed receipts, confirm 40km rule |
| Foreign employment income discrepancies | Treaty vs. non-treaty treatment | Apply treaty provisions correctly, claim foreign tax credits |
