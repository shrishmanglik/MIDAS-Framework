# Chemistry Misconception Database

This reference catalogs common student misconceptions organized by topic area. Each entry includes the misconception, the correction, the grade level where it typically appears, and guidance for using the misconception in question design (especially as MCQ distractors).

---

## Atomic Structure and Bonding

### 1. Electrons Orbit the Nucleus Like Planets
- **Misconception**: Electrons travel in fixed circular or elliptical paths around the nucleus, like planets around the sun.
- **Correction**: Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals) described by quantum mechanics. We can only describe the probability of finding an electron in a particular region of space, not its exact path. Orbitals have specific shapes (s = spherical, p = dumbbell, d = cloverleaf).
- **Grade Level**: 9-11
- **Distractor Use**: "The 2p electron follows an elliptical orbit at a fixed distance from the nucleus"

### 2. Larger Atoms Have More Electron Shells, So They Are Always Bigger
- **Misconception**: Atoms always get bigger as atomic number increases.
- **Correction**: Atomic radius DECREASES across a period (left to right) because increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer, even though the same shell is being filled. Radius increases DOWN a group because new shells are added. Cations are smaller than their parent atoms; anions are larger.
- **Grade Level**: 10-11
- **Distractor Use**: "Chlorine has a larger atomic radius than sodium because it has more electrons"

### 3. Ionic Bonds Are Stronger Than Covalent Bonds
- **Misconception**: Ionic bonds are always stronger than covalent bonds.
- **Correction**: Bond strength depends on specific compounds. Many covalent bonds (e.g., C-C in diamond, N-N triple bond at 945 kJ/mol) are extremely strong. Ionic bond strength depends on charge and ionic radius (lattice energy). The comparison is not meaningful in general terms.
- **Grade Level**: 10-12
- **Distractor Use**: "NaCl has a higher melting point than diamond because ionic bonds are stronger than covalent bonds"

### 4. Electrons Are Shared Equally in All Covalent Bonds
- **Misconception**: Covalent bonding means equal sharing of electrons.
- **Correction**: Only nonpolar covalent bonds (between identical atoms, like H-H, Cl-Cl) have equal sharing. Most covalent bonds are polar -- the more electronegative atom attracts the shared electrons more strongly. The degree of polarity depends on the electronegativity difference.
- **Grade Level**: 10-11
- **Distractor Use**: "The bond in HCl is nonpolar because both atoms share the electron pair"

## States of Matter

### 5. Ionic Compounds Are Always Solid at Room Temperature
- **Misconception**: Ionic compounds exist only as solids.
- **Correction**: Ionic compounds are typically solid at room temperature due to strong lattice forces. However, when melted or dissolved in water, they exist as free-moving ions and conduct electricity. Some ionic liquids exist at room temperature (e.g., certain organic salts with large, asymmetric ions). The key behavior is that ionic compounds conduct when molten or dissolved, not when solid.
- **Grade Level**: 9-11
- **Distractor Use**: "Solid NaCl conducts electricity because it contains ions"

### 6. Dissolving Is a Chemical Change
- **Misconception**: When sugar or salt dissolves in water, a chemical reaction occurs.
- **Correction**: Dissolving is generally a physical change -- the substance can be recovered by evaporation. However, dissolving ionic compounds involves breaking the ionic lattice and hydrating ions, which involves energy changes. Dissolving acids in water (e.g., HCl) does involve chemical change (ionization). The distinction depends on whether new substances are formed.
- **Grade Level**: 9-10
- **Distractor Use**: "Dissolving NaCl in water is a chemical change because ions are formed"

### 7. Gas Molecules Are Far Apart and Stationary
- **Misconception**: Gas particles are spread out and mostly still, or move slowly.
- **Correction**: Gas molecules move rapidly at high speeds (hundreds of meters per second at room temperature). The average speed of N2 at 25 degrees C is approximately 515 m/s. They are far apart relative to their size (average separation is about 10 molecular diameters at atmospheric pressure), which is why gases are compressible.
- **Grade Level**: 9-11
- **Distractor Use**: "Gases exert pressure because their molecules are heavy"

## Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry

### 8. Balancing Equations Changes the Subscripts
- **Misconception**: To balance a chemical equation, you can change the subscripts in chemical formulae.
- **Correction**: Only coefficients (the numbers in front of formulae) can be changed when balancing. Changing subscripts changes the identity of the substance (e.g., H2O to H2O2 is a completely different compound). Balancing adjusts the number of molecules or formula units, not the composition of each molecule.
- **Grade Level**: 9-10
- **Distractor Use**: Include an option where the equation is "balanced" by changing a subscript

### 9. The Limiting Reagent Is the One Present in Smaller Mass
- **Misconception**: Whichever reactant has less mass is the limiting reagent.
- **Correction**: The limiting reagent is determined by the mole ratio, not mass. You must convert masses to moles, then compare with the stoichiometric ratio from the balanced equation. A small mass of a light molecule could represent more moles than a large mass of a heavy molecule.
- **Grade Level**: 10-11
- **Distractor Use**: "2g of H2 is the limiting reagent when reacted with 20g of O2 because it has less mass" (actually H2 is in excess)

### 10. Conservation of Mass Means Products Weigh the Same as the Largest Reactant
- **Misconception**: The mass of products equals the mass of the main reactant.
- **Correction**: The total mass of ALL products equals the total mass of ALL reactants (in a closed system). Every atom present in the reactants must be accounted for in the products. Mass is conserved across the entire system, not per-reactant.
- **Grade Level**: 9-10
- **Distractor Use**: Present a mass calculation that ignores one of the reactants

## Thermodynamics and Energetics

### 11. Endothermic Reactions Do Not Happen Spontaneously
- **Misconception**: If a reaction absorbs heat (endothermic), it cannot be spontaneous.
- **Correction**: Spontaneity depends on Gibbs free energy (Delta G = Delta H - T * Delta S), not enthalpy alone. An endothermic reaction can be spontaneous if the entropy increase (T * Delta S) is large enough to overcome the positive Delta H. Example: dissolving ammonium nitrate in water is endothermic but spontaneous due to large entropy increase.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "The reaction is non-spontaneous because Delta H is positive"

### 12. Breaking Bonds Releases Energy
- **Misconception**: Energy is released when bonds break.
- **Correction**: Breaking bonds ALWAYS requires energy input (endothermic). Forming bonds ALWAYS releases energy (exothermic). An exothermic reaction overall means that MORE energy is released in forming product bonds than was consumed in breaking reactant bonds. This is one of the most persistent misconceptions in chemistry.
- **Grade Level**: 10-12
- **Distractor Use**: "The reaction is exothermic because the C-H bonds in methane break, releasing energy"

### 13. Temperature and Heat Are the Same Thing
- **Misconception**: Temperature measures how much heat something has.
- **Correction**: Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of particles. Heat is the transfer of thermal energy between objects at different temperatures. A large body of water at 30 degrees C contains more thermal energy than a small spark at 3000 degrees C. Heat capacity (mass times specific heat) determines how much heat is needed to change temperature.
- **Grade Level**: 9-11
- **Distractor Use**: "The metal at 80 degrees C has more heat energy than the water at 40 degrees C" (without considering mass and specific heat)

## Equilibrium

### 14. At Equilibrium, Reactant and Product Concentrations Are Equal
- **Misconception**: Equilibrium means 50% reactants and 50% products.
- **Correction**: At equilibrium, the RATES of the forward and reverse reactions are equal, not the concentrations. Depending on the value of K, equilibrium may lie far to the right (mostly products, K >> 1) or far to the left (mostly reactants, K << 1). The equilibrium constant tells you the ratio.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "At equilibrium, [N2] = [H2] = [NH3]"

### 15. Adding a Catalyst Changes the Equilibrium Position
- **Misconception**: A catalyst shifts the equilibrium toward products (or increases yield).
- **Correction**: A catalyst increases the rates of BOTH the forward and reverse reactions equally. It helps the system reach equilibrium FASTER but does NOT change the equilibrium position or the value of K. The same final concentrations are reached, just sooner.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "Adding a catalyst to the Haber process increases the yield of ammonia"

## Acids and Bases

### 16. Strong Acids Are More Dangerous Than Weak Acids
- **Misconception**: Strong acids are always more corrosive or dangerous than weak acids.
- **Correction**: "Strong" and "weak" refer to the degree of ionization, not concentration or danger. A concentrated weak acid (e.g., concentrated acetic acid, glacial) can be more dangerous than a dilute strong acid (e.g., 0.001 M HCl). Hydrofluoric acid (HF) is a weak acid but extremely dangerous because fluoride ions penetrate tissue and bind calcium.
- **Grade Level**: 10-12
- **Distractor Use**: "HF is less dangerous than HCl because HF is a weak acid"

### 17. Neutralization Always Produces a Neutral Solution (pH 7)
- **Misconception**: When an acid reacts with a base, the resulting solution always has pH 7.
- **Correction**: pH 7 at equivalence only occurs for strong acid + strong base reactions. Weak acid + strong base produces a basic salt (pH > 7). Strong acid + weak base produces an acidic salt (pH < 7). The pH at equivalence depends on the hydrolysis of the salt formed.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "The equivalence point of CH3COOH and NaOH titration is at pH 7"

## Organic Chemistry

### 18. All Carbon Compounds Are Organic
- **Misconception**: Any compound containing carbon is organic.
- **Correction**: Several carbon-containing compounds are classified as inorganic: CO, CO2, carbonates (CaCO3), bicarbonates (NaHCO3), cyanides (KCN), carbides (CaC2), and carbon allotropes (diamond, graphite). Organic chemistry focuses on carbon compounds with C-H bonds and carbon chains/rings, typically involving covalent bonding.
- **Grade Level**: 10-11
- **Distractor Use**: "CO2 is an organic compound because it contains carbon"

### 19. Isomers Have the Same Properties
- **Misconception**: Since isomers have the same molecular formula, they have the same properties.
- **Correction**: Structural isomers can have very different physical and chemical properties. For example, ethanol (C2H5OH, bp 78 degrees C, miscible with water) and dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3, bp -24 degrees C, slightly soluble) are isomers of C2H6O but behave completely differently. Even geometric isomers (cis/trans) differ in melting point, boiling point, and polarity.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "Ethanol and dimethyl ether have the same boiling point because they are isomers"

### 20. Double Bonds Are Twice as Strong as Single Bonds
- **Misconception**: A C=C double bond has exactly twice the energy of a C-C single bond.
- **Correction**: A C=C bond (614 kJ/mol) is NOT twice the strength of a C-C bond (348 kJ/mol). The pi bond component of the double bond (about 266 kJ/mol) is weaker than the sigma bond. This is why double bonds undergo addition reactions -- the pi bond is relatively easy to break while maintaining the sigma bond.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "The C=C bond energy is 696 kJ/mol (2 x 348)"

## Electrochemistry

### 21. Electrons Flow Through the Salt Bridge
- **Misconception**: The salt bridge carries electrons between the two half-cells.
- **Correction**: Electrons flow through the EXTERNAL wire from anode to cathode. The salt bridge carries IONS to maintain electrical neutrality in each half-cell. As the anode dissolves (losing positive ions to solution), negative ions from the salt bridge migrate into the anode compartment. Positive ions migrate into the cathode compartment to replace ions being deposited.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "The salt bridge allows electrons to flow between the half-cells"

### 22. The Anode Is Always Positive
- **Misconception**: The anode is the positive electrode.
- **Correction**: In a galvanic (voltaic) cell, the anode is NEGATIVE (it is the source of electrons). In an electrolytic cell, the anode is POSITIVE (connected to the positive terminal of the battery). The consistent definition is: the anode is where OXIDATION occurs, regardless of charge.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "In a Daniell cell, the zinc electrode is positive because it is the anode"

## Kinetics

### 23. Increasing Temperature Increases the Number of Collisions and That Is Why Reactions Go Faster
- **Misconception**: The main effect of temperature on rate is increasing collision frequency.
- **Correction**: While collision frequency does increase slightly with temperature, the DOMINANT effect is the increase in the fraction of molecules with energy exceeding the activation energy. According to the Arrhenius equation, even a small temperature increase dramatically increases the proportion of effective collisions (those with sufficient energy), which is the primary reason for the rate increase.
- **Grade Level**: 11-12
- **Distractor Use**: "Doubling the temperature doubles the collision frequency and therefore doubles the rate"

### 24. The Order of a Reaction Can Be Determined from the Balanced Equation
- **Misconception**: The coefficients in the balanced equation give the order with respect to each reactant.
- **Correction**: Reaction order MUST be determined experimentally. It equals the stoichiometric coefficients ONLY for elementary (single-step) reactions. For multi-step reactions, the rate law depends on the mechanism (specifically the rate-determining step), not the overall equation. For example, the reaction 2N2O5 -> 4NO2 + O2 is first order in N2O5, not second order.
- **Grade Level**: 12 / IB HL
- **Distractor Use**: "The reaction is second order because the coefficient of A in the balanced equation is 2"

## Usage Guidelines for Question Design

When using misconceptions as MCQ distractors:

1. **One misconception-based distractor per question minimum**: At least one of the three incorrect options should target a documented misconception.

2. **Make it plausible**: The distractor should be the answer a student would get if they held the misconception. Do the calculation "wrong" the way a student would.

3. **Annotate the misconception**: In the question metadata, record which misconception each distractor targets. This enables analytics on which misconceptions are most prevalent.

4. **Vary the misconception level**: Mix foundational misconceptions (from earlier grades) with grade-appropriate ones. Some students carry misconceptions forward.

5. **Use misconceptions in explanation text**: When writing the explanation for the correct answer, address WHY the common misconception is wrong. This turns the question into a learning opportunity.
