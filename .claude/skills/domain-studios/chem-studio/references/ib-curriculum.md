# IB Chemistry Curriculum Reference

## IB Chemistry SL (Standard Level)

Total teaching hours: 150 hours (95 hours core + 40 hours SL/HL + 15 hours practical)

### Topic 1: Stoichiometric Relationships (13.5 hours)
- 1.1 Introduction to the particulate nature of matter and chemical change
  - Atoms of different elements combine in fixed ratios to form compounds
  - Mixtures contain more than one element and/or compound that are not chemically bonded
  - Chemical equations represent chemical change
- 1.2 The mole concept
  - Mole as a fixed number of particles (Avogadro's constant: 6.022 x 10^23)
  - Masses of atoms compared on a relative scale (relative atomic mass, relative molecular mass)
  - Molar mass (M), molar volume of ideal gas at STP (22.7 dm3)
  - Empirical and molecular formulae from composition data and molar mass
- 1.3 Reacting masses and volumes
  - Stoichiometric relationships from balanced equations
  - Mass and gaseous volume relationships in reactions
  - Avogadro's law: equal volumes of ideal gases at same T and P contain equal numbers of molecules
  - Molar concentration of a solution: c = n/V
  - Solution preparation and dilution

### Topic 2: Atomic Structure (6 hours)
- 2.1 The nuclear atom
  - Atoms contain protons, neutrons, electrons; mass number and atomic number
  - Isotopes: same element, different mass number
  - Mass spectrometer: determination of relative atomic mass and abundance of isotopes
- 2.2 Electron configuration
  - Emission spectra evidence for energy levels
  - Main energy levels (n=1,2,3...), sublevels (s,p,d), orbitals
  - Full electron configurations for elements up to Z=36
  - Aufbau principle, Hund's rule, Pauli exclusion principle

### Topic 3: Periodicity (6 hours)
- 3.1 The periodic table
  - Elements arranged by atomic number; periods and groups
  - Relationship between electron configuration and position
- 3.2 Periodic trends
  - Atomic radius, ionic radius, first ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity across a period and down a group
  - Metallic/non-metallic character trends
  - Oxides of Period 3 elements: acid-base character

### Topic 4: Chemical Bonding and Structure (13.5 hours)
- 4.1 Ionic bonding and structure
  - Formation of ionic bonds, electron transfer, lattice structures
  - Physical properties of ionic compounds explained by structure
- 4.2 Covalent bonding
  - Lewis (electron dot) structures, single and multiple bonds
  - Bond polarity and electronegativity difference
  - Coordinate (dative) bonds
- 4.3 Covalent structures
  - Lewis structures including exceptions (expanded octets for Period 3+)
  - VSEPR: prediction of molecular geometry from electron domains
  - Bond angles for linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, octahedral
  - Allotropes of carbon (diamond, graphite, fullerene)
- 4.4 Intermolecular forces
  - London dispersion forces, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding
  - Relationship between intermolecular forces and physical properties (mp, bp, solubility)
- 4.5 Metallic bonding
  - Delocalized electron model, properties of metals

### Topic 5: Energetics/Thermochemistry (9 hours)
- 5.1 Measuring energy changes
  - Enthalpy changes: exothermic and endothermic, Delta H notation
  - Standard enthalpy changes: combustion, formation, neutralization
  - Calorimetry calculations: q = mcDelta T
- 5.2 Hess's law
  - Calculation of enthalpy changes using Hess's law and energy cycles
  - Bond enthalpy calculations (average bond enthalpies)
  - Born-Haber cycles for ionic compounds (HL only)
- 5.3 Bond enthalpies
  - Estimation of enthalpy change from average bond enthalpies
  - Limitations of bond enthalpy calculations

### Topic 6: Chemical Kinetics (7 hours)
- 6.1 Collision theory and rates of reaction
  - Rate of reaction: change in concentration per unit time
  - Factors affecting rate: concentration, temperature, surface area, catalyst
  - Collision theory: activation energy, orientation, Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
  - Catalysts: lower activation energy, provide alternative pathway
  - Rate equations and order of reaction (qualitative at SL)

### Topic 7: Equilibrium (4.5 hours)
- 7.1 Equilibrium
  - Dynamic equilibrium: forward and reverse rates equal in a closed system
  - Equilibrium constant expression (Kc) from balanced equation
  - Le Chatelier's principle: predicting the effect of changes in concentration, temperature, pressure
  - Industrial applications (Haber process, Contact process)

### Topic 8: Acids and Bases (6.5 hours)
- 8.1 Theories of acids and bases
  - Bronsted-Lowry: proton donors and acceptors, conjugate acid-base pairs
  - Lewis theory: electron pair donors and acceptors (HL extension)
- 8.2 Properties of acids and bases
  - Reactions with metals, bases, carbonates
  - pH scale, strong and weak acids/bases
  - Ka and Kb expressions (HL extends with calculations)
- 8.3 The pH scale
  - pH = -log[H+], calculation for strong acids and bases
  - pOH and the relationship pH + pOH = 14 (at 25C)
- 8.4 Strong and weak acids and bases
  - Degree of ionization, conductivity differences
  - Buffer solutions (qualitative understanding at SL)
- 8.5 Acid deposition
  - Causes (SO2, NOx), effects on environment and structures, prevention

### Topic 9: Redox Processes (8 hours)
- 9.1 Oxidation and reduction
  - Oxidation states (rules for assignment)
  - Redox reactions: electron transfer, half-equations
  - Oxidizing and reducing agents
  - Reactivity series of metals
- 9.2 Electrochemical cells
  - Galvanic (voltaic) cells: spontaneous redox reactions producing electricity
  - Standard electrode potential (E_degree), standard hydrogen electrode
  - Cell EMF calculation: E_cell = E_cathode - E_anode
  - Electrolytic cells: non-spontaneous reactions driven by external voltage
  - Applications: electroplating, extraction of metals

### Topic 10: Organic Chemistry (11 hours)
- 10.1 Fundamentals of organic chemistry
  - Functional groups: alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, halogenoalkanes, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines
  - IUPAC nomenclature (up to 6 carbons)
  - Structural and displayed formulae
  - Structural isomers
- 10.2 Functional group chemistry
  - Alkanes: combustion, free radical substitution (mechanism: initiation, propagation, termination)
  - Alkenes: addition reactions (H2, HX, X2, H2O), polymerization, Markovnikov's rule
  - Alcohols: combustion, oxidation, esterification, substitution with HX
  - Halogenoalkanes: nucleophilic substitution (SN1 and SN2 overview)
  - Condensation reactions forming esters and amides

### Topic 11: Measurement and Data Processing (10 hours)
- 11.1 Uncertainties and errors in measurements
  - Random and systematic errors
  - Precision and accuracy
  - Absolute and percentage uncertainty
- 11.2 Graphical techniques
  - Best-fit lines, interpolation, extrapolation
  - Determination of rate/enthalpy from graphs
- 11.3 Spectroscopic identification
  - Mass spectrometry: molecular ion peak, fragmentation patterns
  - IR spectroscopy: characteristic absorptions (O-H, N-H, C=O, C-H, O-H broad)
  - 1H NMR spectroscopy: chemical shift, integration, splitting patterns (HL extends)

## IB Chemistry HL (Higher Level) -- Additional Topics

Total additional teaching hours: 60 hours

### Topic 12: Atomic Structure (HL, 2 hours)
- 12.1 Electrons in atoms
  - Detailed electron configuration using sublevel notation for all elements
  - First ionization energy patterns across periods explained by sublevel theory
  - Successive ionization energies and their relationship to electron shells

### Topic 13: The Periodic Table -- The Transition Metals (HL, 4 hours)
- 13.1 First-row d-block elements
  - Electron configurations of atoms and ions
  - Variable oxidation states, colored compounds (d-d transitions)
  - Complex ion formation, ligand exchange
  - Magnetic properties (paramagnetic vs. diamagnetic)
- 13.2 Coloured complexes
  - Crystal field theory: splitting of d-orbitals in octahedral and tetrahedral fields
  - Spectrochemical series
  - Color and complementary color relationship
  - Factors affecting the color of transition metal complexes

### Topic 14: Chemical Bonding and Structure (HL, 4 hours)
- 14.1 Further aspects of covalent bonding and structure
  - Formal charge, expanded octets (SF6, PCl5, XeF2)
  - Sigma and pi bonding, bond order
  - Hybridization: sp, sp2, sp3 (deduced from electron domains)
  - Delocalization and resonance structures
- 14.2 Hybridization
  - Relationship between hybridization and molecular geometry
  - Delocalized pi systems in benzene, ozone, carbonate ion

### Topic 15: Energetics/Thermochemistry (HL, 7 hours)
- 15.1 Energy cycles
  - Born-Haber cycle for ionic compounds
  - Lattice enthalpy (endothermic, breaking the lattice)
  - Electron affinity, hydration enthalpy, solution enthalpy
  - Entropy changes: Delta S, absolute entropy
  - Gibbs free energy: Delta G = Delta H - T Delta S
  - Spontaneity and the relationship between Delta G, Delta H, Delta S, and temperature

### Topic 16: Chemical Kinetics (HL, 7 hours)
- 16.1 Rate expression and reaction mechanism
  - Rate equation: rate = k[A]^m [B]^n
  - Order of reaction (0, 1, 2) from experimental data
  - Rate constant units for different orders
  - Graphical methods to determine order (concentration-time and rate-concentration graphs)
  - Mechanisms: rate-determining step, molecularity
  - Arrhenius equation: k = Ae^(-Ea/RT), graphical determination of Ea

### Topic 17: Equilibrium (HL, 4 hours)
- 17.1 The equilibrium law
  - Equilibrium constant Kc from balanced equations, Kp for gaseous equilibria
  - Relationship between Kc and Kp
  - Calculation of equilibrium concentrations using ICE tables
  - Reaction quotient Q vs. K to predict direction of reaction
  - Gibbs free energy and equilibrium: Delta G = -RT ln K

### Topic 18: Acids and Bases (HL, 10 hours)
- 18.1 Lewis acids and bases
  - Lewis definition: electron pair donor/acceptor
  - Examples of Lewis acid-base reactions
- 18.2 Calculations involving acids and bases
  - Kw = [H+][OH-] = 1.0 x 10^-14 at 25C
  - pH calculations for strong/weak acids and bases
  - Ka and pKa calculations, percent ionization
  - Buffer solutions: Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, buffer capacity
  - Salt hydrolysis and pH of salt solutions
  - Acid-base titration curves, indicators, equivalence point

### Topic 19: Redox Processes (HL, 6 hours)
- 19.1 Electrochemical cells
  - Nernst equation: E = E_degree - (RT/nF)ln(Q)
  - Concentration cells
  - Electrolysis: quantitative aspects (Faraday's laws), factors affecting products
  - Rechargeable batteries, fuel cells in detail
  - Corrosion as an electrochemical process

### Topic 20: Organic Chemistry (HL, 12 hours)
- 20.1 Types of organic reactions
  - Nucleophilic substitution: SN1 vs. SN2 (mechanisms, factors affecting pathway)
  - Electrophilic addition: mechanism for alkenes
  - Electrophilic substitution: mechanism for benzene (nitration, halogenation)
  - Elimination: E1, E2, Zaitsev's rule
  - Reduction reactions: NaBH4, LiAlH4
  - Oxidation: KMnO4, K2Cr2O7
- 20.2 Synthetic routes
  - Multi-step synthesis planning (retrosynthetic analysis)
  - Functional group interconversions
  - Stereoisomerism: geometric (E/Z) and optical (R/S), chirality, enantiomers, racemic mixtures

### Topic 21: Measurement and Analysis (HL, 2 hours)
- 21.1 Spectroscopic identification of organic compounds
  - Combined use of MS, IR, 1H NMR, and 13C NMR for structure determination
  - Degree of unsaturation (index of hydrogen deficiency)
  - High-resolution mass spectrometry

- 6-12 page report
- Assessed on: personal engagement, exploration, analysis, evaluation, communication
- Must involve at least one independent variable and appropriate data collection
