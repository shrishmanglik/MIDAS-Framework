# Ontario Chemistry Curriculum Reference

## Grade 11 Chemistry -- SCH3U (University Preparation)

**Prerequisite**: Science, Grade 10, Academic (SNC2D)

### Strand A: Scientific Investigation Skills and Career Exploration
- A1: Scientific Investigation Skills
  - Initiating and planning: formulating questions, identifying variables, designing procedures
  - Performing and recording: safely conducting investigations, collecting data
  - Analysing and interpreting: organizing data, identifying patterns, drawing conclusions
  - Communicating: reporting findings using appropriate terminology and conventions
- A2: Career Exploration
  - Identifying careers related to chemistry, describing the education required

### Strand B: Matter, Chemical Trends, and Chemical Bonding
- B1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Assess social/environmental impact of chemical research and technologies (e.g., new materials, pharmaceuticals)
- B2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Investigate periodic trends through experiments and simulations
  - Use Lewis structures and molecular models
- B3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Atomic structure: Bohr model, quantum mechanical model (orbitals, energy levels)
  - Electron configuration (up to Z=20 in detail, Z=36 in abbreviated form)
  - Periodic table organization: periods, groups, blocks
  - Periodic trends: atomic radius, ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity
  - Chemical bonding: ionic, covalent (polar/non-polar), metallic
  - Lewis structures and VSEPR theory (shapes of molecules)
  - Intermolecular forces: London dispersion, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding
  - Relationship between bonding/structure and physical properties (melting point, boiling point, solubility, conductivity)

### Strand C: Chemical Reactions
- C1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Assess impact of chemical reactions on society (combustion, industrial processes, environmental effects)
- C2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Balance chemical equations, perform stoichiometric calculations
  - Conduct quantitative investigations involving reactions (titrations, gas collection)
- C3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Types of chemical reactions: synthesis, decomposition, single displacement, double displacement, combustion
  - Predicting products of reactions
  - Balancing chemical equations including polyatomic ions
  - The mole concept: Avogadro's number, molar mass, molar volume
  - Stoichiometry: mole-to-mole, mass-to-mass, mass-to-volume calculations
  - Limiting reagent and percent yield
  - Solution chemistry: concentration (mol/L), dilutions, solution stoichiometry

### Strand D: Quantities in Chemical Reactions
- D1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Assess economic and environmental implications of industrial chemical processes
- D2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Design and conduct experiments to determine empirical/molecular formulae
  - Solve stoichiometric problems involving limiting reagent and percent yield
- D3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Percent composition by mass
  - Empirical and molecular formulae from experimental data
  - Stoichiometric relationships in balanced equations
  - Limiting reagent calculations
  - Percent yield and actual vs. theoretical yield

### Strand E: Gases and Atmospheric Chemistry
- E1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Assess environmental and health effects of gases (greenhouse gases, air pollutants, CFCs)
  - Evaluate technologies for reducing atmospheric pollution
- E2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Collect and measure gases, verify gas laws experimentally
  - Investigate atmospheric chemistry issues through research
- E3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Kinetic molecular theory of gases
  - Gas laws: Boyle's law (P vs V), Charles's law (V vs T), Gay-Lussac's law (P vs T), combined gas law, ideal gas law (PV = nRT)
  - Dalton's law of partial pressures
  - Molar volume at STP and SATP
  - Gas stoichiometry: volume-volume and mass-volume calculations
  - Atmospheric chemistry: composition of atmosphere, greenhouse effect, ozone layer, acid deposition
  - Simple hydrocarbons as fuels: combustion products, environmental considerations

### Strand F: Solutions and Solubility
- F1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Assess impact of solubility on water quality, environmental contamination
- F2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Prepare solutions of known concentration
  - Conduct qualitative analysis to identify ions in solution
- F3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - The dissolving process: solute, solvent, solution, factors affecting solubility
  - Types of solutions: unsaturated, saturated, supersaturated
  - Solubility curves: temperature effects
  - Concentration: mol/L, mass/volume percent, ppm
  - Dilution calculations: C1V1 = C2V2
  - Net ionic equations, spectator ions
  - Qualitative analysis: solubility rules for common ionic compounds
  - Acid-base reactions: neutralization, titration (basic introduction)

## Grade 12 Chemistry -- SCH4U (University Preparation)

**Prerequisite**: Chemistry, Grade 11, University Preparation (SCH3U)

### Strand A: Scientific Investigation Skills and Career Exploration
- A1: Scientific Investigation Skills (same framework as SCH3U but at increased sophistication)
  - Experimental design with controlled variables
  - Error analysis: random vs. systematic, percent error
  - Advanced data analysis and graphical interpretation
- A2: Career Exploration
  - Careers requiring university-level chemistry (chemical engineering, pharmacy, environmental science, research)

### Strand B: Structure and Properties of Matter
- B1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Evaluate applications of atomic theory (medical imaging, nuclear energy, spectroscopy)
- B2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Use spectroscopy data and models to investigate molecular structure
  - Build molecular models, predict geometries
- B3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Quantum mechanical model of the atom: orbitals, energy levels, electron configuration
  - Electron configurations for all elements including transition metals
  - Orbital diagrams, exceptions (Cr, Cu)
  - Periodic trends (review and extension): successive ionization energies, electron shielding, effective nuclear charge
  - Advanced bonding: sigma and pi bonds, hybridization (sp, sp2, sp3)
  - Molecular polarity: dipole moments, vector addition
  - Advanced intermolecular forces and their effects on physical properties
  - Solid state structures: ionic, molecular, covalent network, metallic crystals

### Strand C: Organic Chemistry
- C1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Assess social and environmental impact of organic compounds (polymers, pharmaceuticals, pesticides, fuels)
- C2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Name organic compounds using IUPAC nomenclature
  - Build molecular models, identify functional groups
  - Carry out organic chemistry experiments (synthesis, identification)
- C3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Hydrocarbons: alkanes, alkenes, alkynes (naming, structure, properties, reactions)
  - Isomerism: structural isomers, geometric isomers (cis-trans / E-Z)
  - Functional groups: alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, ethers, amines, amides, halogenoalkanes
  - Naming compounds with functional groups (IUPAC nomenclature up to 10 carbons)
  - Reactions of organic compounds: addition, substitution, elimination, condensation, oxidation, reduction
  - Polymers: addition and condensation polymerization, examples (polyethylene, nylon, polyester, proteins, carbohydrates)
  - Applications: pharmaceuticals, plastics, fuels, food chemistry

### Strand D: Energy Changes and Rates of Reaction
- D1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Evaluate technologies involving energy transformations (fuel cells, batteries, calorimetry)
  - Assess impact of reaction rates on industrial processes and environment
- D2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Calorimetry experiments (enthalpy of reaction, neutralization, dissolution)
  - Investigate factors affecting reaction rates experimentally
  - Construct enthalpy diagrams and energy profiles
- D3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Enthalpy (Delta H): exothermic and endothermic reactions
  - Calorimetry: q = mcDelta T, enthalpy of reaction per mole
  - Hess's law: calculation using formation enthalpies and multiple reaction steps
  - Standard enthalpies of formation, combustion, neutralization
  - Bond energies: estimating enthalpy changes from bond dissociation energies
  - Kinetics: rate of reaction, factors affecting rate (concentration, temperature, surface area, catalyst, nature of reactants)
  - Collision theory: activation energy, effective collisions, Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
  - Rate laws (qualitative): order of reaction from experimental data
  - Catalysts: how catalysts work, enzymes as biological catalysts
  - Energy profiles: activation energy, transition state, catalyzed vs. uncatalyzed

### Strand E: Chemical Systems and Equilibrium
- E1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Evaluate applications of equilibrium (industrial processes: Haber, Contact, Solvay)
  - Assess environmental issues related to equilibrium (acid rain, dissolved CO2)
- E2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Investigate equilibrium systems experimentally (Le Chatelier demonstrations)
  - Solve quantitative equilibrium problems using ICE tables
- E3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Dynamic equilibrium: characteristics, evidence, conditions for establishment
  - Equilibrium constant expression: Kc, Ksp, Ka, Kb, Kw
  - Le Chatelier's principle: predicting effects of changes in concentration, temperature, pressure, volume
  - Equilibrium calculations using ICE tables
  - Solubility equilibrium: Ksp, predicting precipitation, common ion effect
  - Acid-base equilibrium:
    - Strong vs. weak acids and bases
    - Ka, Kb, Kw relationships
    - pH calculations for strong acids/bases and weak acids/bases
    - Titration curves: strong-strong, strong-weak, weak-strong
    - Indicators: choosing appropriate indicators from pKa
    - Buffer solutions: composition, Henderson-Hasselbalch equation (conceptual understanding)
  - Gibbs free energy: Delta G = Delta H - T Delta S (introductory level)
  - Relationship between Delta G and K (qualitative)

### Strand F: Electrochemistry
- F1: Relating Science to Technology, Society, and the Environment
  - Evaluate applications of electrochemistry (batteries, fuel cells, electroplating, corrosion prevention)
  - Assess environmental impact of electrochemical processes (mining, recycling batteries)
- F2: Developing Skills of Investigation and Communication
  - Build and test electrochemical cells
  - Investigate factors affecting cell voltage
  - Conduct electrolysis experiments
- F3: Understanding Basic Concepts
  - Oxidation-reduction reactions: electron transfer, oxidation numbers
  - Half-reactions: oxidation and reduction half-reactions
  - Balancing redox equations using half-reaction method (in acidic and basic solutions)
  - Electrochemical cells: galvanic (voltaic) cells, cell notation, cell diagrams
  - Standard reduction potentials: standard hydrogen electrode (SHE), using tables to predict cell voltage
  - Cell voltage calculation: E_cell = E_cathode - E_anode
  - Spontaneity: positive E_cell indicates spontaneous reaction
  - Electrolytic cells: electrolysis of molten salts and aqueous solutions
  - Faraday's laws: quantitative relationships (Q = It, n = Q/nF)
  - Applications: electroplating, industrial electrolysis (chlor-alkali process), metal refining
  - Corrosion: electrochemical nature, prevention methods (galvanization, sacrificial anode, coatings)
  - Batteries: primary (alkaline, zinc-carbon) and secondary (lead-acid, lithium-ion)
  - Fuel cells: hydrogen fuel cell, advantages and challenges
