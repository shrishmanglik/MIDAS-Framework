# Objection Library Reference

> Common sales objections and proven response frameworks. Every objection is a buying signal — the client is telling you what they need to hear before they can say yes.

---

## Response Frameworks

### Framework 1: Feel-Felt-Found

**Use for**: Emotional objections (fear, uncertainty, skepticism, doubt)

**Structure**:
1. **Feel**: "I understand how you feel..." (validate the emotion)
2. **Felt**: "Others in your position felt the same way..." (normalize the concern)
3. **Found**: "What they found was..." (share the resolution)

**Example**:
> "I completely understand your concern about the price. Several of our clients felt the same way when they first saw the investment. What they found was that the compatibility feature paid for itself within 8 months through increased premium conversions — and after that, it's pure upside."

---

### Framework 2: Reframe

**Use for**: Objections based on a wrong assumption or narrow perspective

**Structure**:
1. Acknowledge the objection
2. Reframe it from a different angle
3. Show why the reframed view is more accurate

**Example** (reframing price as investment):
> "That's a fair point about the price difference. Let me reframe the comparison: AstroSage charges $8,000 for a compatibility score. We charge $22,000 for a compatibility engine that includes AI interpretation, branded reports, and sub-200ms API performance. The question isn't which is cheaper — it's which one drives more premium conversions on your platform."

---

### Framework 3: Bridge

**Use for**: Valid objections that should not be deal-breakers

**Structure**:
1. Acknowledge the valid concern
2. Bridge to a solution or mitigation
3. Redirect to the bigger picture

**Example**:
> "You're right that 8 weeks is longer than you'd hoped. Here's how we can bridge that: we can get your team sandbox access in Week 1 so they can start experimenting immediately, and we can launch Phase 1 (basic compatibility scores) by Week 4 — giving your users value while we complete the AI interpretation and report customization."

---

### Framework 4: Evidence

**Use for**: Factual or technical objections that can be answered with data

**Structure**:
1. Acknowledge the question
2. Present specific data or proof
3. Connect the evidence to the client's situation

**Example**:
> "Great question about accuracy. We use Swiss Ephemeris — the same astronomical calculation library used by NASA. Our beta users rated calculation accuracy 4.7 out of 5, and we use Lahiri Ayanamsa, the standard recognized by the Indian government. We also make our methodology transparent so knowledgeable users can verify calculations independently."

## Objection Categories

### Category 1: Price Objections

#### "It's too expensive"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Value justification — they need to defend this spend internally |
| **Framework** | Reframe + Evidence |
| **Response** | Reframe from cost to investment; show ROI calculation; present build-vs-buy comparison |
| **Key data points** | ROI payback period, cost per outcome, competitor comparison, build-in-house estimate |
| **Fallback** | Offer reduced scope pilot at lower price, payment terms, or annual discount |

#### "We don't have the budget"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Could be real budget constraint OR a polite way of saying "not convinced" |
| **Framework** | Feel-Felt-Found + Bridge |
| **Response** | First, determine if it's a real budget issue or a value issue. If budget: offer phased approach or payment terms. If value: revisit the ROI case. |
| **Key question** | "If budget weren't a constraint, would this be the right solution for you?" |
| **Fallback** | Phased implementation, deferred payment, reduced initial scope |

#### "Competitor X is cheaper"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Seeking validation that the premium is justified |
| **Framework** | Reframe + Evidence |
| **Response** | Never badmouth the competitor. Instead, highlight what you include that they don't. Focus on outcomes, not features. |
| **Key data points** | Feature comparison, performance benchmarks, integration effort, total cost of ownership |
| **Fallback** | Match on one dimension (setup fee, first month free) in exchange for longer commitment |

#### "Can you do it for less?"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Testing if there's negotiation room |
| **Framework** | Bridge |
| **Response** | "Our pricing reflects the value delivered. I can explore what a reduced scope would look like at a lower price point — or I can offer [payment terms / annual discount] at the current scope. Which would be more helpful?" |
| **Rule** | Never reduce price without reducing scope or getting something in return |

### Category 2: Trust / Credibility Objections

#### "We've never heard of you"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Risk — buying from an unknown vendor feels risky |
| **Framework** | Evidence + Feel-Felt-Found |
| **Response** | Share specific proof: case studies, user metrics, technology credentials, partnerships. Offer a pilot to reduce perceived risk. |
| **Key data points** | User count, accuracy ratings, technology stack credentials, testimonials |
| **Fallback** | Risk-free pilot with defined success criteria |

#### "How do we know this will work?"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Fear of failure — they need proof before committing |
| **Framework** | Evidence |
| **Response** | Present case studies with quantified results. Offer a pilot program with defined success metrics. Show technical proof (accuracy, uptime, performance benchmarks). |
| **Fallback** | Pilot program with success-based pricing or performance guarantee |

#### "What if the technology doesn't perform?"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Technical risk |
| **Framework** | Evidence |
| **Response** | Present technical specifications (uptime SLA, response time, accuracy ratings). Offer sandbox access for their team to test before committing. Reference the technology foundation (Swiss Ephemeris, established algorithms). |
| **Fallback** | Sandbox trial period, SLA with penalties, performance guarantee |

---

### Category 3: Timing Objections

#### "Now isn't a good time"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Could be genuine (other priorities) or avoidance (not interested enough) |
| **Framework** | Bridge |
| **Response** | "I understand you have competing priorities. Two questions: Is this something you'd want to do in the next quarter? And what would need to change for the timing to be right?" This separates genuine timing issues from polite declines. |
| **Key question** | "What would need to be true for this to move to the top of the priority list?" |
| **Fallback** | Offer to start with a planning phase that requires minimal client resources |

#### "Our timeline is shorter than yours"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Urgency — they need results faster |
| **Framework** | Bridge |
| **Response** | Propose a phased launch: Phase 1 (core feature) delivered fast, Phase 2 (customization, polish) follows. Show a compressed timeline with trade-offs clearly stated. |
| **Fallback** | Accelerated timeline with premium pricing or reduced initial scope |

#### "Let's revisit next quarter"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Delay tactic — often means "I'm not ready to decide" |
| **Framework** | Bridge + Evidence |
| **Response** | "Absolutely — I'll follow up in [month]. Before we wrap up, can I share one data point? Every month without this feature, you're leaving an estimated $[X] in premium conversions on the table. Starting now means you're [X] months closer to ROI by next quarter." |
| **Key question** | "What would you need to see in the next 2 weeks to make a decision before next quarter?" |

---

### Category 4: Technical / Product Objections

#### "We can build this ourselves"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Control, long-term cost, NIH (Not Invented Here) syndrome |
| **Framework** | Reframe + Evidence |
| **Response** | Present a build-vs-buy TCO comparison: developer hours, astronomical data licensing, AI model training, ongoing maintenance, opportunity cost of pulling engineers off core product. Typically, building in-house costs 3-5x more than buying, and takes 6-12 months longer. |
| **Key data points** | Developer cost estimate ($150-200/hr * X hours), time to market comparison, ongoing maintenance cost, opportunity cost |
| **Fallback** | Offer hybrid: they own the integration, we provide the engine |

#### "We need feature X that you don't have"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Capability gap — is it a deal-breaker or a nice-to-have? |
| **Framework** | Bridge |
| **Response** | "That's a great request. Let me understand: is [feature X] a must-have for launch, or something that would be valuable in a future phase? If it's critical, let me check with our team on feasibility and timeline. If it's a phase 2 item, we can include it in the roadmap." |
| **Key question** | "If we could deliver [feature X] in phase 2, would the current feature set be sufficient for launch?" |

#### "What about data privacy / security?"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Compliance, user trust, liability |
| **Framework** | Evidence |
| **Response** | Present security architecture: data encryption in transit and at rest, access controls, data processing agreements, GDPR compliance approach, data retention policies. Offer to have a technical security call with their security team. |
| **Key deliverable** | Security whitepaper or architecture document for their review |

---

### Category 5: Decision-Making Objections

#### "I need to check with my [boss/board/team]"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | They may not be the decision-maker, or they need internal ammunition to justify the purchase |
| **Framework** | Bridge |
| **Response** | "Of course — this is a significant decision. How can I help you make the case internally? I can prepare a one-page executive summary, an ROI calculation, or join a call with your [boss/board] to answer questions directly." |
| **Key action** | Arm the champion: give them a leave-behind document that sells the deal for them |

#### "We're also evaluating other vendors"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Due diligence — they want to make sure they're choosing the best option |
| **Framework** | Evidence |
| **Response** | "That's smart — you should compare options carefully. I'd encourage you to evaluate on three criteria: [1] quality of output (not just feature checklist), [2] technical fit with your platform, and [3] total cost of ownership including integration effort. I'm confident we'll compare well on all three. Would a side-by-side comparison document be helpful?" |
| **Key deliverable** | Competitive comparison matrix (factual, not attack-based) |

#### "We need to think about it"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Unresolved concern they haven't articulated |
| **Framework** | Feel-Felt-Found |
| **Response** | "Absolutely — take the time you need. Can I ask: what's the main thing you want to think through? I may be able to provide information that helps your decision." This surfaces the hidden objection. |
| **Key question** | "On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you that this is the right solution? What would move you from a [their number] to a 9?" |

---

### Category 6: Risk / Change Objections

#### "What if it doesn't integrate well with our platform?"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Technical risk, wasted investment |
| **Framework** | Evidence + Bridge |
| **Response** | Offer sandbox access for their engineering team to evaluate before committing. Present API documentation and sample code. Reference integration time estimates from previous implementations. Include integration support hours in the proposal. |
| **Fallback** | Technical proof-of-concept phase before full commitment |

#### "Our team is too busy to implement this right now"

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Underlying concern** | Resource constraint — they want it but can't staff it |
| **Framework** | Bridge |
| **Response** | "I hear that — your team has a full plate. Two options: we can provide enhanced integration support (more engineering hours) to reduce the load on your team, or we can start with Phase 1 (API setup and sandbox) so your team can integrate when bandwidth opens up." |
| **Fallback** | Extended timeline, managed integration service, or phased approach |

## Objection Response Checklist

Before delivering any objection response:

- [ ] The objection is stated in the client's own words (not paraphrased into something easier to answer)
- [ ] The underlying concern is identified (not just the surface objection)
- [ ] The response begins with acknowledgment or empathy (not defense or argument)
- [ ] An appropriate framework is used (Feel-Felt-Found, Reframe, Bridge, Evidence)
- [ ] Supporting evidence is specific and credible (data, case studies, testimonials)
- [ ] A second-level response is prepared for push-back
- [ ] A fallback position is defined (concession + what we get in return)
- [ ] The response sounds natural when read aloud
- [ ] No competitor is badmouthed in any response
- [ ] No promises are made that cannot be kept
